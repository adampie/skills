# Ranking sources

The general rule from `SKILL.md` is that the primary record beats anything
written about it. What counts as primary depends on the domain.

| Domain | Primary | Acceptable | Discard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Software releases | Release notes, changelog, git history, mailing list threads, issue tracker | Maintainer blog posts, established trade press | Tutorial farms restating the changelog |
| Science | The paper itself, preprint plus its published version | University press release, specialist reporting | Popular science summaries of a summary |
| Law and regulation | Statute text, court judgment, regulator publication | Law firm analysis, legal press | News reports of a decision without the citation |
| Companies | Filings, earnings statements, official announcements | Reporting quoting named sources | Aggregators, stock-tip sites |
| Statistics | The statistical agency's own release and tables | Reporting that links the table | Charts with no cited source |
| Current events | Wire services and outlets with named reporting | Established outlets citing the wires | Social posts, anonymous aggregators |
| Biography | Official records, first-party statements, the subject's own account | Obituaries in outlets that check, encyclopaedic entries with citations | Fan wikis, celebrity aggregators |

## Signals a source is worthless

- The domain exists to rank for a keyword: a topic in the hostname plus a
  generic suffix (`-expert`, `-helper`, `-hub`, `-guide`).
- The page restates a better source without linking to it.
- No author, no date, no corrections policy.
- Text with the rhythm of model output: a listicle of headings, each two
  sentences long, saying the same thing.
- The figure appears nowhere but on sites that plainly copy each other.

Two content farms agreeing is one source counted twice.

## Encyclopaedias

Wikipedia is a finding aid, not a citation. Use it to locate the primary
source in its references, then cite that. Its own text can be cited only when
the claim is about what the encyclopaedia says.

## Age

A source's date matters more than its rank for anything phrased in the present
tense. A 2019 paper is primary and current for a historical claim, and stale
evidence for "the fastest available today". Check whether a newer edition,
revision or superseding release exists before citing the one search returned
first.

## Paywalls and dead links

Cite what was actually read. When only a headline and abstract were visible,
say the verdict rests on the abstract. An archived copy is acceptable if the
archive URL is the one cited. Never cite a link that failed to fetch.
